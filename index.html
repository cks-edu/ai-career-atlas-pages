Help & getting started

A short guide to courses, practice, saved progress and study tools.

Start with one small result

  1. Start with the core path. Open AI Learning Path and begin Course 01 if AI is new to you. AI courses and technical/nontechnical career guides are available in the AI library.
  2. Choose a level. Begin with Beginner when the subject is new. Read the prerequisites before moving to Intermediate or Advanced.
  3. Follow the lesson sequence. Read the explanation, work through the mental model, try the task and check its expected result.
  4. Check and revisit. Try the quiz, open a hint when needed and use My learning to return to your saved courses.

Common questions

Do I need an account?

No. All courses and core learning tools work in guest view. An optional local learning profile saves your name or nickname, routine and reading preferences in this browser. It is not a private login or a cloud-synced account. AI Learning Path stays available to guests and learners with a local profile. Use its resume action to return to your last lesson; My learning remains in the profile menu. On a shared device, other people using the same browser can see saved records.

What do the three levels mean?

Beginner introduces the foundations. Intermediate connects concepts and handles edge cases. Advanced examines trade-offs, failures and larger projects. Some focused Production AI modules expose one implemented entry level. These are learning levels, not job seniority or professional certifications. Use each course’s prerequisites and evidence checks to decide where to begin.

Where are labs, quizzes, projects and career preparation?

Open a course and choose Lessons. Use the topics outline to select a topic or subtopic, then follow Learn, Practise, Check and Resources. Collapse topics for more reading space. Course menu holds the full lab, tools, project, interview, résumé, job links and milestone details. Course Focus keeps the lesson visible with a small timer; Collapse timer hides the clock, while Close Focus or Escape pauses it and returns to the same lesson. Existing concepts and practice remain available at all three levels.

How does progress work?

Opening a page does not complete it. Record the practice you actually finished and use the course’s evidence checks. Quiz results are recorded under the selected tutor learner; course practice and notes belong to this browser’s journey. Weekly progress shows the retained quiz, Focus and scheduled-review evidence, not a guarantee of competence or employment.

How do I back up or move my learning?

Use Save / Restore Progress in the top bar for one unified JSON backup of your journey, tutor records, notes, reviews, local profile and Focus history. Merge import retains existing records and conflicting notes for recovery. Keep both backups until you have checked the result. A weekly report is a readable export, not an importable backup. Browser clearing, private browsing or blocked storage can remove or prevent local saves.

Is the AI tutor required or free?

Study help begins with authored course explanations, hints, quizzes and lesson links. The optional Qwen lesson finder runs on your device after you enable it and download the model. It has no inference-server or token bill; downloads, memory, graphics hardware, battery and compatibility still matter. Suggestions can be wrong, so check the linked lesson and official sources. No cloud model is silently substituted.

What does Focus mode record?

The classroom can keep a Focus timer beside your lesson. An optional black screen and a quiet review-task view help reduce distractions. You can pause, hide the timer and exit with the button or Escape. Finishing a timed session can record a timebox; it does not prove attention, mark a lesson complete or block other applications.

How do Google Calendar reminders and missed reviews work?

Open Google Calendar updates in the tutor-review planner or Weekly progress. When available, connect your Google account to update the same managed review events after saving or rescheduling. Updates run while this website is open; reconnect when access expires. Manual event drafts and calendar-file imports remain snapshots, and old copies need a one-time cleanup. Calendar attendance and later edits made in Google do not complete lessons or change this planner.

Are all tools and cloud labs free?

The course tools list identifies open-source, commercial and mixed options, with alternatives and official sources. Local tools can still use hardware and electricity. Cloud services, vendor accounts, GPUs, commercial sandboxes or optional products can cost money. Read a lab’s setup, limits and cleanup steps before running it. A local example is not a certified production deployment.

What does Trending mean?

The symbol marks dated editorial selections related to published skill trends. Its link opens the supporting report. It does not represent this site’s enrollment counts, a universal course ranking or a promise of a job. The catalog includes source dates and the selection method.

A source link is blocked or a lesson seems incorrect. What can I do?

Some official documentation sites require a normal browser session, sign-in or an anti-bot check. Try the vendor’s documentation or learning portal and check the product version. Keep a note of the course, level, lesson and source URL. You can report a content issue through the public website repository; avoid including private study notes, credentials or employer data. GitHub may require sign-in.

How to use this evidence

Technical documentation establishes how systems work. Employer surveys describe expectations. Neither establishes hiring demand for every specialization. This guide therefore does not rank roles by demand, growth, salary or future job security.

The Career Explorer covers 50 career families and 15 niche specializations across technical and nontechnical backgrounds. The engineering planner and practice handbooks cover 12 deeper technical tracks. These are overlapping learning paths, not an official occupation taxonomy.

Terms that change the decision

Use responsibilities and system boundaries to interpret unfamiliar job titles.

Validate the market you can actually enter

  1. Choose your location, work authorization, seniority and remote/on-site constraints.
  2. Collect recent, distinct job descriptions from employer career pages. Record URL and date; remove duplicates and expired roles.
  3. Group by responsibilities: model lifecycle, serving, cloud platform, security, retrieval, application delivery or customer architecture.
  4. Count recurring required skills and distinguish them from optional tools. Compare years of experience, degree requirements and on-call expectations.
  5. Choose a first project that addresses a recurring requirement, then discuss its evidence with a hiring manager or practitioner.

This is an editorial research method, not a completed local vacancy survey. No country-specific or India-specific demand conclusion is asserted. Certifications are optional evidence of a vendor scope; inspect current requirements before spending money.

Source library & review method

Foundational research plus tool and cloud documentation. Each record states its scope and limitations. Links need internet; the planner and source summaries work offline.
